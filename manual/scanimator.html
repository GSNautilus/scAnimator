Start here

Quick start

scAnimator runs in a web browser. Nothing to install: open the live demo and the PBMC 3k dataset starts moving.

  1. Open the demo. Go to gsnautilus.github.io/scAnimator in a current Chrome, Edge or Firefox (WebGL2 is needed). The landing page lists the datasets; click PBMC 3k.
  1. Watch it warp. The cells are already moving: the embedding sweeps from n_neighbors 5 to 150 and back. The label at the top says where it is.
  2. Turn it around. Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom. 360 makes the camera circle on its own.
  3. Colour it. Open Color By in the panel on the left and pick Cell Type. Click a name in the legend to hide those cells.
  4. Look up a gene. Open Gene Expression, type LYZ and press Enter: monocytes light up.
  5. Make it glow. In Render Settings, raise Point Size and Bloom Strength.
  6. Record. Set a duration, press Record, and the viewer renders one exact loop to a ZIP of PNG frames (p. 00).
Good to know
  • Everything happens in your browser. A dataset you load from a folder is never uploaded anywhere.
  • Genes are fetched one at a time as you ask for them, so even a large dataset opens quickly.
  • ↺ (bottom right) puts the camera back if you get lost.

The idea

What the animation shows

A UMAP plot is one answer to a question with a dial on it. scAnimator turns the dial and lets you watch.

UMAP places cells so that each one stays near its n_neighbors most similar cells. With a small value it only cares about very close neighbours, so fine, local structure shows and the map can break into many islands. With a large value it balances each cell against more of the dataset, so the broad, global arrangement shows and small groups merge. Neither picture is the true one. Watching one become the other shows which clusters are robust and which only appear at one setting.

The same ~39,000 neural cells at five n_neighbors values, same camera. At 150 one population splits off as a separate island.

How the frames are made

  1. Keyframes. build_viewer.py runs a 3D UMAP at a set of n_neighbors values, for example 11 values from 5 to 150 (--sweep 5-150-11), all from the same PCA input.
  2. Alignment. Each UMAP is free to come out rotated or mirrored. Every keyframe is Procrustes-aligned to the one before it (rotation, reflection and scale only), so whatever still moves is a real change in structure.
  3. In-between frames. A cubic spline through the aligned keyframes gives every cell a smooth path (180 frames by default). Playback runs up the sweep and back down, so it loops.
  4. In the browser. All frames load at once as one binary file; the viewer blends between neighbouring frames, so motion stays smooth at any speed.
Reading it carefully
  • The in-between frames are interpolations for the eye, not UMAP runs. Only the keyframes are real embeddings, which is why the label reads n_neighbors ~ 30: between keyframes the value is approximate.
  • Distances between far-apart islands mean little in any UMAP. What a cell keeps as neighbours through the sweep means more.

Metadata

Colour, filter, normalize

Every categorical column the dataset was built with can colour the cells, filter them, or even out their numbers.

Color By

ChoiceColours
a metadata columnone colour per category (cell type, cluster, sample…), with a legend
Gene Expressionthe gene loaded in the Gene Expression group, through its colormap
Solid Colorevery cell one colour, picked with the colour swatch that appears
Gradientevery cell cycles through a colormap in time with the timeline
Gradient (Cluster)the same, but each cluster starts at its own place in the colormap, so clusters pulse out of step

Filter

Pick a column, then tick the categories to show. All, None and Invert work on the whole list. Filters on different columns combine: a cell shows only if every column lets it. The filter column does not have to be the colour column: here the cells are coloured by cell type and Leiden clusters 0 and 1 are hidden.

Normalize

Pick a column and press Normalize Cell Counts. Every group is cut down to the size of the smallest by a fixed random pick (the same cells every time), so a large population no longer hides a small one. Legend counts follow. Press it again to see every cell.

Expression

Gene expression

Type a gene and see where it is expressed, as colour, as point size or as brightness, while the embedding keeps moving.

  1. Type a gene symbol. Suggestions appear as you type, most-expressed genes first, with their rank (#42/13714). Use ↑ ↓ and Enter, or click one.
  2. Choose how to show it. Map to: Point Size, Colormap or Intensity.
  3. Adjust the scale. Linear, Log or Quantile, and Expression Intensity to push faint signal up or strong signal down.

Under the controls the viewer says how many cells express the gene (LYZ: 1600/2643 cells expressing). Clear gene goes back to the plain colours. Expression is stored per gene relative to that gene's own maximum: compare genes by pattern, not by brightness.

ControlOptionsWhat it does
Map toPoint Size · Colormap · IntensitySize and Intensity keep the Color By colours and scale size or brightness by expression. Colormap replaces the colours and shows a colour bar.
ColormapViridis, Magma, Plasma, Inferno, Rainbow, Turbo, Coolwarmthe colours for Colormap mapping
Expression ScaleLinear · Log · QuantileLog lifts low values; Quantile spreads cells evenly over the range, good for genes with a few extreme cells
Expression Intensity0.2 – 3.0 (1.0)a gamma on the scaled values: above 1 brightens weak expression

Look

Render settings

Each cell is drawn as a small light: points add up where they overlap, so dense regions glow. These settings shape that light.

SliderRange (start)What it does
Point Intensity0.05 – 1.0 (1.0)brightness of each point. Lower it for dense datasets so overlaps don't burn out to white.
Bloom Strength0 – 4 (0)glow around bright regions (Three.js UnrealBloom)
Point Size2 – 40 (2)size of each point. Points are drawn in perspective, so they grow as you zoom in.
PersistenceOff, 1 – 100how slowly old frames fade: a trail of light behind moving cells
Depth of FieldOff, 1 – 100near cells stay sharp, farther ones blur more and more: a sense of depth
Tracer LengthOff, 1 – 180 frameslines along each cell's recent path, crisp rather than fading
A starting look
  • Small dataset (a few thousand cells): Point Size 30–40, Bloom 0.5, Intensity 0.8.
  • Large dataset (tens of thousands): Point Size 12–18, Intensity 0.5–0.7, Bloom 0.3. More cells need less light each.
  • Persistence and tracers only show while something moves: turn on Morph or 360.

Time and space

Playback and camera

One timeline drives everything that moves, so a loop always comes back to where it started. That is what makes recordings loop seamlessly.

The timeline

The playhead runs for Duration seconds (30 by default) and starts again. Within that time each moving thing completes loops cycles. Drag the playhead to scrub; Pause holds the current frame, and you can still rotate and change anything.

Export

Recording video

Recording does not capture the screen. It steps through the timeline frame by frame, so every frame is exact at any resolution, however slow the computer.

  1. Set up the view. Colours, gene, render settings, camera angle. Turn on Morph and/or 360.
  2. Choose the loop. Duration is the length of the video; loops how many sweeps or turns fit in it. Pick the frame rate.
  3. Choose the frame. Square crops to a centred square; Native records at the window's size, 2K and 4K render square frames of 2160 or 3840 pixels.
  4. Press Record. The timeline restarts and renders Duration × fps frames. It stops by itself at the end of the loop (or press Stop Rec) and downloads umap_warp_frames_60fps.zip: numbered PNG frames and fps.txt.

Making an MP4

Unzip the frames and turn them into a video with ffmpeg (PowerShell or any shell, in the unzipped folder):

ffmpeg -framerate 60 -i frame_%05d.png -c:v libx264 -crf 16 -pix_fmt yuv420p umap_warp.mp4

Use the number in fps.txt for -framerate. Because the loop ends where it started, the MP4 loops seamlessly too. Frames are written straight into the ZIP (ZIP64), so even long 4K recordings don't run out of memory.

SettingOptionsNotes
fps24, 30, 60, 120frames per second of the result, not of the screen
Duration · loops1–600 s · 1–99shared with playback (p. 00)
Squareon (default) / offcentred square crop, for social media and slides
Legendon (default) / offdraws the legend (or gene colour bar) into the frames
ResolutionNative, 2K, 4K2K and 4K render off-screen at that size, independent of the window

Tell a story

Storyboards

A storyboard is a short JSON file: a list of views, each with a caption. Present it live with a clicker, or render it to MP4 clips.

Each step is a view: what to colour by, which cells to show, which n_neighbors frame to hold. Between steps the viewer transitions: cells fade in and out, colours cross-fade, and if the n_neighbors changes the embedding morphs. The camera keeps turning throughout. A step can also be a slide, a full-frame card of text and an image that fades over the cloud.

{
  "dataset": "pbmc3k",
  "render": { "point_size": 40, "brightness": 0.8, "bloom": 0.5 },
  "camera": { "zoom": 1.2, "elevation": 15 },
  "steps": [
    { "name": "overview", "color_by": "cell_type", "n_neighbors": 5,
      "caption": "PBMC 3k", "subcaption": "2,643 blood cells, by cell type" },
    { "name": "lymphocytes", "filter": { "cell_type": ["CD4 T", "CD8 T", "NK", "B"] },
      "caption": "Lymphocytes" },
    { "name": "nkg7", "color_by": "gene:NKG7", "colormap": "magma", "caption": "NKG7" }
  ]
}

The full example is examples/pbmc3k_tour.json in the repository.

Present it

Load it with the film-strip button, by dropping the .json (and any slide images) onto the viewer, or with viewer.html?dataset=pbmc3k&storyboard=<url>. Each step holds, turning, until you press →. ↺ leaves the storyboard and puts your own settings back.

Render it

python scripts/render_storyboard.py examples/pbmc3k_tour.json            # MP4 clips + _full.mp4
python scripts/render_storyboard.py examples/pbmc3k_tour.json --stills   # one frame per step: a quick check

The script drives the viewer in Edge (Playwright) frame by frame and encodes with ffmpeg: per step a transition clip and a seamlessly looping hold clip. --draft renders half size.

For performance

Experimental

Two effects made for projection and live visuals rather than analysis.

Audio reactivity

Tick Enable Audio Input and allow the microphone (or pick another input device, such as a loopback of your music). The sound is split into three bands, each with a level meter, and each band drives one property of every cell.

BandRangeDrives (start)
Bass20 – 250 HzPoint Size
Mids250 Hz – 4 kHzIntensity
Highs4 – 20 kHzColormap

Each band can map to Point Size, Intensity, Colormap or Displacement (cells jitter out of place), and has a Scale curve (linear, log to boost quiet sound, exponential to boost peaks), Sensitivity, Strength, Smoothing and Spread (how differently cells respond).

  • Peak Hold keeps each band at its last peak and lets it fall slowly.
  • Cluster Mapping gives each cluster of a chosen column one band, in turn: clusters answer different parts of the music.
  • Expression Weight scales the response by the loaded gene: expressing cells react most.

Fractal reflection

Repeats the picture as a kaleidoscope of smaller copies around the centre. Divisions (2–8) sets how many, Opacity how strong the copies are, and Show original in center keeps the full-size cloud on top (picture on p. 00).

Your own dataset

Bring your own data

Any dataset with a PCA (or another embedding) and cell metadata can be animated. One Python script turns it into the files the viewer reads.

From Python (AnnData)

pip install numpy pandas scipy scanpy umap-learn h5py
python scripts/build_viewer.py --input my_data.h5ad --output-dir datasets/my_data --name "My data" --sweep 5-150-11
python scripts/update_dataset_index.py

The UMAP input is obsm["X_pca"] unless you name another embedding (--embedding-key X_scvi); with no suitable embedding, PCA is computed. The colour column is found automatically (cell_type, leiden, louvain…) or set with --cell-type-col. Every categorical obs column becomes a Color By and Filter option unless you list them with --metadata-cols.

From R (Seurat)

source("scripts/export_seurat.R")
export_for_scanimator(obj, "export", cell_type = "cell_type", columns = c("seurat_clusters", "orig.ident"))
python scripts/build_viewer.py --export-dir export --output-dir datasets/my_data --name "My data" --sweep 5-150-11 --metadata-cols cell_type,seurat_clusters,orig.ident

export_seurat.R writes the PCA, the metadata and the normalized expression matrix (sparse, as binary files) that --export-dir reads. It works with Seurat v4 and v5.

Options

OptionDefaultWhat it does
--sweep a-b-nnonen keyframes from n_neighbors a to b. Without it: one static UMAP (fast)
--n-interp-frames180frames between the first and last keyframe
--umap-neighbors15n_neighbors of the static UMAP

--cell-type-colautothe default colouring column

Above 200,000 cells the script reads the .h5ad in backed mode and streams expression through h5py, so atlases of a million cells fit in ordinary memory. A sweep keeps checkpoints, so an interrupted run picks up where it stopped.

Opening it

  • Serve the folder: python -m http.server 8000 in the scAnimator folder, then open http://localhost:8000. After update_dataset_index.py your dataset has its own card.
  • Or load it straight from disk: on the landing page, Load Folder and pick the dataset folder. The files are copied into the browser's own storage (IndexedDB) and stay on your computer. This works on the live demo too.

When something's off

Troubleshooting

Most problems are the browser refusing local files, or a look that suits a different number of cells.

What you seeWhy, and what to do
Stuck on "Loading data"The page was opened as a file (file://): browsers block fetch there. Serve the folder with python -m http.server 8000, or use Load Folder.
"No datasets found"datasets/index.json is missing or empty. Run python scripts/update_dataset_index.py.
Black screen, no cloudWebGL2 is off or blocked. Turn on hardware acceleration in the browser's settings; check get.webgl.org/webgl2.
Tiny dotsPoint size is in perspective: zoom in, or raise Point Size (2 is the minimum).
Everything whiteToo much light: lower Point Intensity and Bloom. Large datasets need much less of both.
Gene not foundSymbols are case-sensitive and must match the dataset (human CD3E, mouse Cd3e). With --hvg-only only variable genes exist.
Gene overlay stays emptyA server without HTTP Range support: the viewer then fetches the whole expression.bin, which can take a while for large data. http.server and GitHub Pages both support Range.
Slow, low fpsDepth of field, tracers and fractal cost the most. Turn them off while exploring; recording speed doesn't depend on fps.
Recording ZIP never appearsVery long 4K recordings take time to assemble after the last frame (Finalizing…). If it reads ZIP FAILED, record fewer frames or a lower resolution.
Audio input does nothingThe browser needs microphone permission, and only grants it on https:// or localhost.
Storyboard refusedThe message names the step and the problem (no column, value or gene of that name) and lists what exists.
